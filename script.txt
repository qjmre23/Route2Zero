ROUTE2ZERO 2.1 — CONCEPT PRESENTATION SCRIPT
20-slide companion to Route2Zero_Concept_Deck_20_Slides.pptx
Team Larpers · AI × City Climate Action Hackathon 2026
Suggested duration: 10–12 minutes, followed by the live prototype

SLIDE 01 — Screen first. Validate before committing.
Say: Route2Zero helps a city decide which Metro Manila jeepney corridors deserve electrification validation first. This exact build screens 1,522 historic route-direction records, finds nine robust priorities, and adds 20 dated current external route records.
Emphasize: Those 20 records confirm a current external map record and observed geometry, not active service or franchise authority.

SLIDE 02 — Why urban transport climate action needs a corridor lens
Say: Electrification succeeds or fails corridor by corridor. Service intensity, electricity conditions, local exposure, operator circumstances, and practical charging constraints differ, so a national target still needs a defensible city workflow.
Transition: The problem is not a lack of ambition; it is fragmented decision evidence.

SLIDE 03 — The planning gap
Say: Route, schedule, population, energy, infrastructure, operator, and finance evidence sit in different systems and at different geographic scales. A single unexplained score would hide that mismatch. Route2Zero keeps the evidence categories, claim status, and missing fields visible.

SLIDE 04 — Decision frame, users, and beneficiaries
Say: The decision is which corridors a city should validate and prioritize first, what evidence could reverse that shortlist, and who must participate. LGUs, DOTr and LTFRB coordinate the process; operators, utilities, finance partners, riders, and affected communities validate the conditions.

SLIDE 05 — Product promise
Say: The system audits the historic universe, constructs evidence-aware route features, estimates one missing service input when necessary, calculates climate and equity screens, tests policy uncertainty, builds a constrained portfolio, and exports an auditable decision pack.

SLIDE 06 — AI and human authority
Say: Machine learning estimates a missing historic service input and groups similar corridors. Deterministic models calculate climate, evidence, sensitivity, and portfolio results. Humans control policy weights and constraints. The language model only explains supplied fields, and its ranking influence remains false.

SLIDE 07 — Data, provenance, and licensing
Say: The build registers 18 sources and controlled ledgers with URLs, dates, licenses, checksums, and limitations. Project code is MIT licensed. OSM data remains ODbL, WorldPop remains CC BY, and the historic GTFS retains its separate DOTC terms.

SLIDE 08 — Current-evidence validation layer
Say: We queried a dated Overpass snapshot using Metro Manila’s documented route equals bus plus bus equals share taxi convention. Twenty one-to-one name and endpoint matches pass the 2023 edit cutoff. Each record stores the relation, date, matching rationale, and observed member-way geometry.
Emphasize: Active status remains uncertain for all 1,522 records.

SLIDE 09 — Deliberate ML restraint
Say: The service model is validated with five grouped folds and beats a median baseline on the historic derived target. It is used for only one route where historic daily vehicle-kilometres are missing: LTFRB_PUJ2451. The model supplies 6,656.52 proxy vehicle-kilometres per day, not ridership or current service.

SLIDE 10 — Corridor typology
Say: A three-cluster K-means model with a 0.373 silhouette separates dense urban trunks, high-stop-density cores, and long regional connectors. Typology supports comparison and sample diversity but contributes no policy points and makes no socioeconomic claim.

SLIDE 11 — Climate and energy impact
Say: For the eight-corridor portfolio, the primary base case is plus 2,710.9 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per year. The bounded range is minus 8,190.9 to plus 22,288.0. The low case turns negative when high electricity use meets a carbon-intensive grid; vehicle efficiency is the strongest sensitivity, followed by grid intensity.

SLIDE 12 — Equity without overclaiming
Say: The current equity field is a WorldPop population-exposure proxy. It is not poverty, vulnerability, accessibility, tenure, or informal-settlement evidence. PSA city-level poverty estimates are registered as the next candidate, but they remain unintegrated until route-to-city geometry and aggregation are validated.

SLIDE 13 — Charging and operator readiness
Say: Mapped substations and charging stations provide proximity context, not verified capacity or site control. Operator desk searches cover all eight Phase-1 corridors and find one named operator reference, but every readiness score correctly remains a neutral prior because fleet, depot, financing, and other consent-based evidence are missing.

SLIDE 14 — Feasibility snapshot, not a budget
Say: The portfolio screen estimates about 1,943 vehicles, 102 charging stations, and 4.91 billion pesos in vehicle-plus-charger capital. Every figure is marked proxy. Financing is missing, and depot, civil, grid, battery, operating, tax, insurance, and finance costs are excluded.

SLIDE 15 — Human-controlled policy model
Say: The default lens assigns 40 percent to climate impact, 30 percent to equity, 15 percent to charging readiness, and 15 percent to operator readiness. Users can compare named lenses, and the scenario ID changes with the policy choice. The evidence itself does not change.

SLIDE 16 — Uncertainty and rank stability
Say: Five thousand fixed-seed tests vary policy weights around the default lens. Nine records remain robust priorities. A top-ten frequency is a sensitivity result, not a probability that the route is correct or ready.

SLIDE 17 — Value of information
Say: For each route, Route2Zero perturbs six unresolved evidence fields and reruns the same deterministic selection logic. The result is a practical queue showing which observation could move rank or change portfolio membership most.

SLIDE 18 — Constrained Phase-1 portfolio
Say: The eight-corridor portfolio is not a copied top-eight list. City coverage, corridor direction, equity, evidence, and inactivity constraints replace four simple-top-ranked records with four better validation cases. There is no budget constraint, and the separate cost screen is not used to optimize selection.

SLIDE 19 — Flagship corridor deep dive
Say: Francisco Homes–Cubao is rank one at 79.07, evidence grade C at 38.34, and 100 percent top-ten across the tested weight scenarios. Its base climate case is plus 368.0 tonnes per year, bounded by minus 1,111.8 to plus 3,025.3. It remains a historic screening record with current activity unverified.

SLIDE 20 — Pilot pathway and accountable team
Say: The six-month pilot moves through governance, data refresh and ground truth, locked model validation, climate and feasibility calibration, co-designed policy choices, and a city-owned handover. Team Larpers assigns owners across product, data, climate, experience, model evaluation, GIS, stakeholder validation, and governance.
Close: Route2Zero does not ask a city to trust a rank. It shows what to validate, why it matters, and when the evidence is strong enough to decide.
