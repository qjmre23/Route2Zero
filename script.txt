ROUTE2ZERO 2026 DEMONSTRATION SCRIPT
24-slide companion to Route2Zero_Demonstration_Deck.pptx
Prepared for the AI × City Climate Action Hackathon 2026
Recommended run time: 10–12 minutes plus live demonstration

SLIDE 01 — Route2Zero
Say: Route2Zero is transparent corridor intelligence for a just e-jeepney transition. It screens 1,522 route-direction records and turns them into a shortlist that city teams can inspect, challenge and validate.
Emphasize: This is decision support. It does not authorise procurement, finance or route changes.

SLIDE 02 — The decision question
Say: The product answers one practical question: which corridors deserve deeper electrification work first? It creates a consistent screening surface across climate value, equity exposure and feasibility.
Transition: That question is difficult because a rank can hide the wrong trade-offs.

SLIDE 03 — Why this matters
Say: A high-activity corridor may still have weak grid, operator or affordability conditions. Equity proxies can also be overinterpreted. Route2Zero keeps the components, sources and confidence boundaries visible.
Emphasize: Operators and riders remain part of the decision.

SLIDE 04 — Planning friction
Say: GTFS activity, population density, grid context and operator evidence are held at different scales by different institutions. The product turns these fragments into an evidence-acquisition workflow ending in a city-owned shortlist.

SLIDE 05 — Product promise
Say: Route2Zero is transparent enough to challenge and useful enough to act on. Scenario choices are visible, missing evidence stays missing, and every report records the active lens.

SLIDE 06 — How it works
Say: The pipeline audits the route universe, constructs a screening geometry, builds four interpretable signals, applies visible weights, then ranks and exports the result.
Demo cue: Point to the scenario controls before showing any route.

SLIDE 07 — Four-signal score
Say: The default policy lens is 35 percent emissions activity, 35 percent equity density, 15 percent grid feasibility and 15 percent operator readiness. The interface normalises live weights to 100 percent.

SLIDE 08 — AI boundary
Say: Ranking is deterministic. Optional AI can translate a result into plain language or summarise evidence requests, but it cannot write to score columns or change rank order.
Emphasize: A deterministic fallback keeps the product usable without an AI key.

SLIDE 09 — Live dashboard
Say: The 2026 dashboard is a responsive Netlify-ready decision cockpit. The left rail controls the city and policy scenario. The centre presents the decision signal, confidence notice, map and action queue. PDF, Word and CSV exports sit beside the controls.

SLIDE 10 — Mapbox road geometry
Say: When a route is selected, the browser requests a detailed Mapbox Directions path so the line follows streets rather than flying between distant stops. Clustered records keep the regional view readable.
Emphasize: The path is a visualisation aid and still requires operational validation.

SLIDE 11 — Scenario testing
Say: A decision-maker can move all four weights, include or exclude reduced-confidence records and apply a city lens. The action queue refreshes immediately without modifying the underlying evidence.

SLIDE 12 — Current action queue
Say: Under the default policy lens, the leading record is Francisco Homes–Cubao. The table shows emissions, equity, grid and operator components beside the composite score.
Demo cue: Click a row and show that the map and selected recommendation update together.

SLIDE 13 — Leading result
Say: Francisco Homes–Cubao scores 71.4 overall, with an emissions activity proxy of 98.2 and an equity density proxy of 77.4. The grid value is a coarse regional proxy and operator readiness remains the neutral 50 placeholder.
Emphasize: The rank prioritises feasibility work; it is not an investment recommendation.

SLIDE 14 — Decision pack
Say: PDF produces a print-ready scenario report, Word creates an editable briefing and CSV hands the ranked data to analysts. Each export records the current weights and city lens.

SLIDE 15 — Responsive access
Say: The dashboard has been verified at a 390-by-844 mobile viewport with no horizontal overflow. The responsive map still displays the street-following route and confidence notice.

SLIDE 16 — Reproducible architecture
Say: Source fields move through an auditable pipeline: GTFS audit, route activity, WorldPop catchment, Luzon grid context and composite outputs. Provenance and confidence fields remain attached to final records.

SLIDE 17 — What the MVP can prove
Say: The route universe and deterministic score are reproducible. One thousand five hundred twenty-one records have all four scoring dimensions. Two records have source GTFS shapes; the remaining geometries are labelled approximations.

SLIDE 18 — Visible limitations
Say: The GTFS service calendar ends in 2020. WorldPop measures density, not settlement status. Grid feasibility is regional, not depot-specific. Operator readiness is a placeholder until verified.
Emphasize: Each limitation becomes a pilot task rather than a footnote.

SLIDE 19 — Validation workflow
Say: The six-month pilot begins with city scoping, then operator interviews, route ground-checks and grid/finance screening before a validated shortlist is accepted.

SLIDE 20 — Six-month strategy
Say: Month one establishes governance and the evidence baseline. Months two to four acquire and reconcile local evidence. Month five co-designs the policy lens. Month six produces the accountable decision pack.

SLIDE 21 — Milestone gates
Say: Gate one approves the evidence plan. Gate two confirms route validation. Gate three accepts the city shortlist. A route may remain visible at lower confidence, but it cannot silently become decision-ready.

SLIDE 22 — Team accountability
Say: The team assigns clear owners across product, data, mobility, engineering, policy, research, engagement and quality assurance. Final roles and availability are confirmed at pilot kickoff.

SLIDE 23 — Success measures
Say: The pilot is judged by decision quality: complete evidence cards, three LGU validation sessions, at least 80 percent ground-check coverage for priority records and one accepted decision pack.

SLIDE 24 — The ask
Say: Help Route2Zero turn a screening MVP into a city-owned electrification roadmap. Pilot with three LGUs, validate with operators and riders, and publish a transparent corridor shortlist.
Close: Screen, validate, co-design, act.
